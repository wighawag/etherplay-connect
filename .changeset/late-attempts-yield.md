'@etherplay/connect': patch
---

A slow attempt no longer lands over something newer. The auto-connect at construction (restoring the stored account for `targetStep: 'SignedIn'`, the last wallet for `'WalletConnected'`, the chosen wallet for `'WalletChosen'`), an explicit `connect` to a wallet, `selectWallet` and `requestSignature` each publish only after the wallet answers, and they used to publish over whatever had happened while they waited. With a wallet slow to answer `eth_chainId`/`eth_accounts` and holding several accounts, a stored account A could be restored over a fresh sign-in as B, after which `useCurrentAccount` followed the wallet back to B without a signature and left the app with no signer. A late failure of the same attempt rested the flow at `Idle` and tore the newer session's wallet down, a signature A gave could sign B in with a key derived from A's signature (and save it), and a slow `selectWallet` could drop a connected wallet back to `WalletChosen`. Each now notes how far the flow had moved when it started and, after every await and on its failure path, drops its result if anything moved the flow since: a newer `connect` or `selectWallet` starting, a different step, account or mechanism, or `loading` ending (a `disconnect`). The newer attempt always wins.

Three more answers that arrive late are now dropped instead of being applied to whatever the connection has become. `unlock()`: a refused prompt answered after a `disconnect` no longer republishes the old wallet onto `Idle`, and an unlock answered after switching wallets no longer judges the new wallet by the old one's accounts. The lock poll: a locked answer from the previous wallet no longer marks a newly connected wallet `locked` for good. The sign-in popup: a popup that completes after the app connected a wallet no longer signs its account in over that wallet (tearing the wallet down and saving the hosted account), matching what its failure path already did.

Visible consequence: an app that calls `connect` or `ensureConnected` before a slow auto-connect has restored the stored session now gets the attempt it asked for, rather than having it overwritten by the restore. With `requestSignatureAutomaticallyIfPossible`, that attempt asks for a fresh signature.
